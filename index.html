Ten works · Basel · mmxxvi

The ten works listed here were begun between February and August of 2026, in Basel. They are instruments rather than products: a training monitor written in C that reads temperatures straight out of the Linux kernel; a transformer trained on twelve thousand piano performances; a departure board on my desk that asks the Swiss railways when the next train leaves.

I read computer science at the University of Basel, after a year of mechanical engineering at ETH Zürich. Each entry below is set out the way a catalogue sets out a painting — what it is made of, when it was begun, and where it is kept.

  1. I
    Medium
    Pure C (C99). Linux sysfs, ANSI escape sequences. No dependencies.
    Begun
    August 2026
    Kept
    HerRei/train-tui

    train-tui

    A terminal dashboard for watching a training run. It reads clock speeds, memory, power draw and temperature directly out of /sys/class/drm for AMD cards, and asks nvidia-smi for the rest. No Python, no ncurses — only escape sequences written to a file descriptor, which is why it starts instantly and survives an SSH session that drops.

  2. II
    Medium
    Tauri, Svelte, Python & PyTorch. Desktop application.
    Begun
    n.d.
    Kept
    Private collection

    LocalSR

    A desktop application for enlarging photographs with neural super-resolution models, run entirely on the machine in front of you. Images larger than memory are processed in overlapping tiles and blended back at the seams; on macOS it watches memory pressure and retreats before the system begins to swap. ICC colour profiles survive the round trip, which is the part usually left out.

    Visit LocalSR — image studies & desktop alpha

  3. III
    Medium
    PyTorch on Apple Silicon. Twelve-layer transformer, 12,000 MIDI performances.
    Begun
    March 2026
    Kept
    HerRei/gpt2-piano-mps-12k

    A transformer taught to play the piano

    A GPT-2 sized model trained from nothing on twelve thousand classical piano performances, tokenised with Miditok's remi scheme so that bar, position, pitch, duration and velocity each become a word it can learn. Give it a phrase and it continues. The whole training run happened on a laptop, on Apple's Metal backend rather than a rented cluster.

    See the piano roll Read the source

  4. IV
    Medium
    ESP32-WROOM-32 and an ST7789 panel, 240 × 320. C++ on FreeRTOS.
    Begun
    February 2026
    Kept
    HerRei/Sbb_Tracker_Sissach

    A departure board for Sissach

    A physical object: it sits on a desk and shows the next trains out of Sissach. Every half minute it asks transport.opendata.ch what is coming, filters the JSON while it is still arriving so the parser never needs more memory than the microcontroller has, and redraws a small colour panel. A FreeRTOS watchdog resets the board when the network stack wedges, which it does.

    Microcontroller
    ESP32-WROOM-32
    Display
    ST7789 2.0″ IPS, 240 × 320, over SPI
    Recovery
    FreeRTOS task watchdog, 8 s
    Parser
    ArduinoJson, filtered stream
    Draw
    about 140 mA at 5 V

  5. V
    Medium
    Java 21, JavaFX. A hand-written TCP protocol, four players.
    Begun
    May 2026
    Kept
    HerRei/PhantomHunt

    PhantomHunt

    Three players against one in a labyrinth, written for cs108 at Basel. The game is the visible part; underneath it is a protocol — an authoritative server pushing state to four clients over sockets, a lobby that parses commands and private messages, and roles that rotate over four rounds so nobody keeps the advantage. Covered by JUnit, measured by JaCoCo.

    A tool for asking, in ordinary language, what has changed between two releases of the Canton of Basel-Stadt nature inventory. A language model writes the spatial query; a validation layer refuses anything that is not strictly read-only before it is allowed near the database. The answer comes back as a map.

Biographical notice

The author

Hermès Nathan Reisner. I live in Sissach, half an hour up the line from Basel, and read computer science at the University of Basel — after a year of mechanical engineering at ETH Zürich, which is where I learned to prefer things that have to survive contact with something physical.

What interests me is the seam where software meets the machine: kernel interfaces, GPU registers, firmware that has to recover from its own faults, and the protocols that keep separate programs honest with one another. I would rather write two thousand lines of C that start in a millisecond than assemble a framework that starts in four seconds.

I am glad to hear about research collaborations, systems work, or anything with hardware attached to it. The curriculum vitae is here.
