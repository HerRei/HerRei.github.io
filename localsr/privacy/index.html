How LocalSR handles files, diagnostics, downloads and support, on your computer and in the phone app.

In short

LocalSR never uploads your photos or videos. The desktop app makes only the network requests in this table, each one with a switch, and all of them can be changed in Settings > Privacy & updates at any time. The phone app makes none.

Why the count exists. LocalSR is made by one person in their free time. The count answers a few practical questions: how many people use each version, which platforms and graphics engines are actually in use, and whether an update has reached people. That decides where the work goes, for example whether a platform still needs testing or a fix needs to reach more people. It cannot tell who you are, what you upscale or how often you use the app.

What LocalSR never does. No uploads of your media, no account, no advertising, no tracking across apps or websites, no selling or sharing of data, no profiles, and no training on your photos. The app's source code is public, so all of this can be checked: the count is a single request in updates.rs, and the counting server is in packaging/hosting/stats.

Local processing

LocalSR processes the images and videos you choose on your computer. It does not upload them for enhancement or use them to train models. There is no account and no advertising, and crash reports are never sent automatically. Since LocalSR 0.5, nothing goes over the network on a fresh install until you finish the first-launch setup. The only usage figure the app sends is the optional anonymous update-check count described under Network connections, and you can turn it off.

The app reads your files and metadata, creates previews and writes separate results. The local queue stores file paths, thumbnails, media properties, settings, job status and output paths. Recipes, benchmarks, models, temporary files and recovery backups are also local. Closing the app does not erase them.

LocalSR for iPhone and Android

The phone app processes the photos you pick or share to LocalSR on your phone. It does not upload photos, has no account and sends no usage figures, crash reports or other diagnostics. Nothing in this app goes over the network: the Android app has no internet permission, and the iPhone app contains no network code. The update check, the anonymous count and the crash reports described below belong to the desktop app only.

  • What the app reads: the photos you pick in the photo picker or share to LocalSR, with their date and location metadata, which it copies into the upscaled photo.
  • What it stores: copies of the photos you picked or shared, the upscaled results and small previews in the app’s own storage, plus your settings and the result of the one-time chip check. Delete All in the app removes the copies and results; uninstalling the app removes everything.
  • What it writes: a new photo in your photo library (iPhone: Photos, with add-only access; Android: Pictures/LocalSR), only when you tap Save. Your original is not changed.
  • Backups: on iPhone the app keeps its results in its cache folder, which iCloud Backup does not include. Android cloud backup is turned off for LocalSR.
  • Links: the Licences and Privacy Policy links open your browser; this website then receives ordinary connection information, as described under Support and website.

Network connections

First-launch setup. On a fresh install, LocalSR 0.6 opens with a two-page setup. The first page shows the three data switches (automatic update checks, the anonymous count and crash reports), the second the look of the app, the menu bar or tray icon and the completion sound. The three data switches start on and are shown in their real state before anything is sent; nothing goes over the network until you choose Done, and installs that already made a choice keep it. You can change every switch later in Settings > Privacy & updates. (LocalSR 0.5 had a one-page setup with Continue and the same three switches in its menus.)

Update check and anonymous count. When enabled, at launch the app reads its update feed on GitHub Pages (for the beta, herrei.github.io/localsr/updates/beta.json) on macOS, Windows and Linux. After a check, if the anonymous count is enabled, it sends the app version, platform (update target, such as darwin-aarch64-mps-native) and release channel to the maintainer’s Mac mini at macmini-ci.tail34a4e0.ts.net, with no install ID and no cookies. Versions before 0.5 sent the count on first launch before showing a choice, and versions before 0.4.0 only checked for updates on macOS. Check for Updates… still works manually when automatic checks are off. The Microsoft Store edition does neither. External website and license links open services with their own privacy policies.

Models and face detection. Models download from their authors on GitHub or Hugging Face when you first choose them (LocalSR’s own Quick and Best models from github.com/HerRei/localsr-models), and the face-detection model downloads on first use, the first time a face-aware job runs. Source media is not included in download requests. Once your models are downloaded, LocalSR works offline.

Downloads

Installers, source bundles and in-app update files are downloaded from GitHub Releases. GitHub receives ordinary connection information, including your IP address and the requested file, and publishes a total download count per file. LocalSR reads those totals without information about individual visitors.

The maintainer’s Mac mini keeps a copy of each release at downloads.hermesr.duckdns.org, which the website does not link. Requests there pass through an Infomaniak VPS over an encrypted WireGuard tunnel; HTTPS ends on the Mac mini. The VPS relays encrypted traffic and writes no visitor access log.

The Mac mini receives ordinary connection information, including IP addresses and requested paths. It counts a download once per file, per visitor, per day. To recognize repeat requests on the same day, it combines the IP address and browser or app identification with a random value that changes daily and is deleted after two days. It stores only the resulting hash, never the IP address, and writes no access log. Only daily totals per page, file, app version and referring website are kept.

The website visit and app update-check counts reach the Mac mini through Tailscale Funnel; Tailscale describes its handling in its privacy policy.

Sharing is your choice

Crash reports (from LocalSR 0.5). Crash reports start on for new installs and can be turned off in the first-launch setup or later in Settings > Privacy & updates. When enabled, reports are stored only on your computer·at most 5 reports and for at most 30 days·and are deleted immediately when you turn crash reports off. Paths, file names, e-mail addresses and tokens are redacted. Crash reports are never sent automatically: you can review a stored report and choose to open a prefilled GitHub issue. Each report contains: app version, build target, OS and version, architecture, device kind, task and model, error message, location or exit code, and redacted last error. Native host crashes such as segmentation faults may not be captured.

Report a problem (from LocalSR 0.5). Report a problem… opens a GitHub issue in your browser. Diagnostics are previewed and redacted before you choose to open the issue, and nothing is sent until you submit it on GitHub.

Copy diagnostics and benchmarks. Copy diagnostics puts a hardware and app summary on your clipboard when requested, omitting media names, paths and content. Benchmark reports can be saved locally. Neither is automatically sent. Review logs, screenshots and pasted reports before sharing; they can contain information you add or error details. Clipboard synchronization and operating-system backups follow your system settings.

Storage and removal

Direct editions use ~/Library/Application Support/LocalSR on macOS, %LOCALAPPDATA%\LocalSR on Windows, and ${XDG_DATA_HOME:-~/.local/share}/LocalSR on Linux. Windows Store data can also be virtualized beneath its package's LocalCache folder. Settings and recipes live under next; model checkpoints live under models. Exports use the chosen output folder.

Removing a queue item does not delete its original or completed exports. Backups can retain older queue information. For complete removal, quit LocalSR, keep a backup of anything needed and delete the relevant local data and exports. This also removes settings, recipes and models. The tested Windows Store uninstall deleted its package profile. Back up that profile before uninstalling if you want to retain it. A verified backup restored it after reinstallation. Other packaging routes may leave local data behind.

Support and website

Public GitHub issue reports, usernames and attachments are visible to others. For private contact and privacy requests, email hermes.reisner@gmail.com. Email is received in the project's Gmail inbox and is not automatically posted to GitHub. Do not send credentials or signing keys.

The website uses GitHub Pages and locally hosted assets. A small script counts visits: it sends the page path and, if you followed a link from another website, that website’s name to the Mac mini. It sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser and does not run when your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control. Pages work without it. GitHub records visitor IP addresses for security. See GitHub's privacy statement and Pages visitor information.

The publisher is Hermes Reisner, with HerRei as project branding. Contact the address above to request removal of information you sent for support.

Responsibility and your rights

Who is responsible. Hermes Reisner, Basel, Switzerland, hermes.reisner@gmail.com, is responsible for the processing described here. LocalSR is a free, non-commercial project.

Why. Counting visits, downloads and update checks, and keeping the servers secure, serve a legitimate interest: knowing roughly how many people use each version, and delivering the app and its updates safely. The counts are kept only as daily totals that cannot identify anyone. For visitors in the EU and EEA the legal basis is Article 6(1)(f) GDPR; under Switzerland’s Federal Act on Data Protection the processing follows its general principles. Support emails and issue reports are processed to answer them.

Who else receives data. GitHub (website, downloads and issue reports), Infomaniak (the encrypted relay to the download copy), Tailscale (the address used for visit and update-check counts), Hugging Face and GitHub (model downloads you start) and Google (the Gmail inbox). Some of these providers are in the United States and describe their safeguards for international transfers in their own privacy statements. Nothing is sold, and no profiles or automated decisions are made.

How long. Daily hashes and their random values are deleted after two days; daily totals are kept. Support emails are kept while they are needed to handle your request and are deleted on request.

Your rights. You can ask what data about you is processed, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted. You can object to the counting: turn off the update-check count in the app, or enable Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control in your browser. Because only unidentifiable totals are stored, most counts cannot be traced back to you. If you think your data is handled unlawfully, you can complain to the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC/EDÖB) or, in the EU, to your national data protection authority.
